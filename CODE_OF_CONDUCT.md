<h1>What-If Machine Community Code of Conduct</h1>

**Professional discussion · Evidence-first collaboration · Respect for people and ownership**

## Our purpose

The What-If Machine community exists to discuss reproducible engineering
problems, verification methods, model behavior, and responsible improvements
to the project. Productive participation is grounded in technical evidence and
respectful treatment of every person involved.

## Community standards

| Principle | Expected behavior |
|---|---|
| 🤝 **Respect** | Address ideas and evidence without attacking, humiliating, or dismissing people. |
| 🔎 **Evidence** | Provide reproducible steps, relevant logs, and clear distinctions between observation and assumption. |
| 🎯 **Relevance** | Keep issues, reviews, and discussions focused on the project and the topic being examined. |
| 🔐 **Privacy** | Remove credentials, personal data, private prompts, private paths, and other sensitive information before posting. |
| ©️ **Ownership** | Respect copyright, attribution, repository restrictions, and the project's proprietary contribution policy. |

## Expected behavior

- Communicate clearly, patiently, and professionally.
- Welcome good-faith questions and technically supported disagreement.
- Critique implementations and claims rather than the people presenting them.
- Correct mistakes openly when new evidence changes the conclusion.
- Share only material you are authorized to disclose.
- Follow repository templates so reports contain enough information to act on.
- Respect moderation decisions and requests to move, redact, or refocus content.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not permitted in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or discriminatory conduct;
- personal attacks, insults, deliberate humiliation, or sustained disruption;
- sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted sexual attention;
- publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information;
- knowingly posting credentials, malware, exploit payloads, or dangerous
  instructions without a legitimate and clearly contained technical purpose;
- spam, impersonation, deceptive attribution, or false claims of endorsement;
- unauthorized copying, redistribution, commercialization, or submission of
  proprietary project material; and
- retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith concern.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions, project documentation, and other spaces officially associated
with What-If Machine. It also applies when a person is explicitly representing
the project in a public setting.

## Reporting concerns

For a non-sensitive project concern that can safely be discussed in public,
open an issue with the minimum information necessary to explain the problem.

For harassment, threats, doxxing, private evidence, or other sensitive matters,
do **not** publish the details in an issue. Contact the repository owner through
an available private method or use GitHub's built-in content-reporting tools.
For an immediate threat, contact the appropriate local emergency service.

All reports should be made in good faith. Deliberately false or retaliatory
reports may themselves violate this Code of Conduct.

## Enforcement

Matthew Newland, as repository owner and project maintainer, is responsible for
interpreting and enforcing these standards. Depending on the circumstances,
responses may include clarification, a request for correction, content removal,
a warning, temporary participation restrictions, a permanent ban, or referral
to GitHub or appropriate authorities.

Reports will be reviewed as promptly and impartially as reasonably possible.
Privacy will be respected to the extent compatible with safety, investigation,
platform rules, and applicable law.
